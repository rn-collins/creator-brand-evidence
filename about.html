About

Brand decisions are business decisions. They deserve more rigor than taste alone.

Too much brand and creator work is aesthetic, opaque, trend-driven, or disconnected from how businesses actually make decisions. This studio exists to change that—by treating brand and creator strategy as a research, trust, governance, and risk discipline.

Built for regulated, expert-led, and creator-scaling brands where credibility is part of the product. The outcome: brand and creator decisions with visible reasoning, graded evidence, and a paper trail you could defend to a regulator, a board, or a skeptical customer.

Why this approach exists

Evidence, judgment, and honesty about uncertainty.

Most brand work fails because the reasoning is invisible—founder preference, internal politics, or trend imitation dressed up as strategy. The alternative is to make the evidence explicit, grade its strength, name what we don’t know, and test where we can.

That’s especially important in trust-sensitive, regulated, and expert-led categories, where bad messaging, vague claims, or poor disclosure can create real harm—not just weak conversion. A brand that operates in health, legal, financial, or cannabis-adjacent space is making claims a regulator can review, working with creators whose disclosures a platform can audit, and building trust an audience can lose in a single bad post. Treating that as a taste problem instead of a governance problem is how avoidable harm—and avoidable liability—happens.

“Evidence-based” doesn’t mean every decision gets a randomized trial. It means naming what kind of evidence a decision actually rests on—customer research, competitor pattern, behavioral literature, internal performance data, or founder judgment—so a client can see the difference between a load-bearing recommendation and an informed bet, and choose how much risk to take on each one.

How the lens applies

Each discipline does specific work in an engagement.

Neuroscience & psychology → trust diagnostics

Reading parasocial dynamics, message framing, and cognitive load is how the audit tells the difference between a creator who builds trust and one who is quietly borrowing it.

Compliance & law → risk and disclosure review

Claims review, endorsement law, and platform terms turn “this feels risky” into a specific, prioritized finding—disclosure, testimonial, affiliate, or substantiation risk named and ranked.

Product management → governance systems

SOPs, approval workflows, and measurement dashboards exist because a good recommendation that no one operationalizes doesn’t change anything six months later.

Research method → evidence grading

Literature review and primary-source synthesis are what make it possible to label a recommendation high-confidence, test-advised, or exploratory instead of just asserting it.

Operating principles

What this studio will and won’t do.

  • Name the evidence, not just the recommendation. Every claim in a deliverable is traceable to a source: customer data, competitor pattern, research literature, or stated founder judgment.
  • Grade confidence explicitly. High-confidence, test-advised, and exploratory recommendations are labeled differently—see the Method page for how.
  • Governance is part of the deliverable, not an afterthought. A brand system that depends on one person remembering the rules isn’t a system.
  • No invented proof. If evidence for a claim doesn’t exist yet, the deliverable says so and proposes how to get it—rather than papering over the gap.
