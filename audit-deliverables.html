Why this format

A deck of impressions isn’t a diagnosis.

Most creator or brand “audits” hand back a slide deck of observations with no way to act on them. These eight deliverables exist because each answers a different operational question: what is true (report), what supports it (evidence map), what could go wrong (risk map), who to work with (scorecard), what to track (measurement framework), what to do first (priority matrix), in what order (roadmap), and how to talk it through with the person who has to decide (debrief). Strip out any one of those and a real recommendation becomes an opinion again.

What a finding looks like

Specific enough to act on, not just flag.

A Risk Map entry doesn’t read “disclosure could be improved.” It reads closer to: affiliate links in three recent posts are disclosed only in a first comment or a bio-link page—below the “clear and conspicuous” bar most FTC guidance and platform policy expect—rated moderate risk, fix-now priority, with a rewritten caption template attached. The Priority Matrix then tells you whether that sits in fix-now, formalize-next, test-before-scaling, monitor, or stop-doing—so the debrief is a decision session, not a re-read of the report.
